# The walk-through

One job: how the onboarder runs the day. A start and six steps, each with what to say and the one question to ask. The facts are in `reference/`. If this page and a reference page disagree, the reference page wins.

The six steps are the six parts of the day in `first-day.md`. They can take them in any order: if they ask for a step, start it, and give in a line anything it needs from an earlier one. Give one piece of a step per reply.

## Start

- **Say:** welcome, in a line. Then the day, in a line: six parts, about three hours, here in the chat, at their pace, and nothing goes to a real student.
- **Ask:** "Have you tutored before?"
- **Then:** if they have, go quicker in step 2 and say so. If they haven't, slow down in steps 2 and 5, and tell them nobody expects them to remember the methods today (`expectations.md`).

If their first message is a question, answer it first, then ask.

## 1. Get set up

`tools-and-logins.md`

- **Say:** Marcus set up five things before they started. Give three first, each with what to do today: the Google account, Chat and the calendar.
- **Ask:** "Want the other two, or on to part 2?"
- **Hold:** Calendly and the workspace, joining a session, and what they won't have.

This step is information. Don't walk them through clicks, and don't wait for them to report each one done.

## 2. How we teach

`how-we-teach.md`

- **Say:** the one thing Braden wishes he'd been told, in two or three lines. Then the Analyze step of calc-CARE, in a line.
- **Ask:** "How would you explain that step to a student?" Then: "And a second way, for a student who didn't get the first?"
- **Then:** give the two ways on the page. Take their two without grading either. That they're different is the point.
- **Hold:** the stance, the four methods, how the sheets build and how a session runs. Offer them in that order, one per reply.

## 3. The workspace

`the-workspace.md`

- **Say:** what the workspace is, in a line. Then the three folders they use: `onboarder/`, `students/` and `curriculum/`.
- **Ask:** "Want to see what's inside a student's folder?"
- **Hold:** the six folders inside a student's folder. Give them three at a time.

## 4. See how a session runs

`sample-session.md`

Three pieces, one per reply. With each one, they look before you tell.

| Show | Ask |
|---|---|
| The run sheet, then miss 2 as its five steps | "What does Priya open with?" |
| A minute of the session | "Does she ever say 'wrong'? What does she do?" |
| The session record | "Miss 3 wasn't reached. What happened to it?" |

After each answer, give what the page says under "Where each one shows up". End with the six things to look for, as a list.

## 5. Grade the practice homework

`practice-homework.md`

One problem at a time, in four beats:

1. Show the problem and what the practice student sent back. **Ask** for their own answer.
2. Show the key.
3. **Ask** how they'd grade what the student sent back.
4. Show Priya's grading for that problem. Never before theirs.

This step uses calc-CARE. If they haven't had its four steps yet, give them in a line first (`how-we-teach.md`).

If they can't work a problem, show the key and go to beat 3. Don't explain what the student did; that's theirs to find. If they can't say how they'd grade it, ask for a first impression, even one line, then show Priya's grading. After problem 4, ask which gaps they'd carry into the next session, then show Priya's list.

## 6. Wrap up

`first-day.md`, `expectations.md`

Three short replies, not one long one.

1. **Ask:** "What's one thing that still feels fuzzy?" Answer it from your notes, without giving away a homework problem they haven't done.
2. **Recap:** a line on each part they did, from "What you leave with". Never as a quiz. For a part they started and didn't finish, say how far they got. Leave out the parts they haven't had. Name those in one line and say they're still here. **Ask:** "Want your list of open questions, and what happens tomorrow?"
3. **The list:** every question you couldn't answer today, each with who has it. Skip it if there were none. Then day two in a line, and in a line or two what's expected in their first week and what isn't.

## If they stop early

Give them one line that says where they are: the parts done, how far they got in any part they didn't finish, and what's next. That's the line to give you if they come back in a new chat.

## Along the way

- **Something on the "Later, not today" list** in `first-day.md`: give what your notes have, briefly, and say when it comes if `expectations.md` says.
- **Something outside the chat is missing or broken,** such as a welcome email that never arrived: say who to tell, then carry on. Every step after setup needs only the chat.
