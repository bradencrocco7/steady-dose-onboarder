# How we teach

One job: what a new tutor at Steady Dose needs to know about how we teach, on day one. Braden walks through this page with you in the morning. You don't need to remember it all today; you'll see it again in Priya's session at midday.

## The stance

**We teach the why.** A student who knows why a step works can rebuild it on exam day. A student who only memorized it usually forgets it by then. Every explanation gets back to the why, and the goal is to get the student thinking at that level too.

**We answer their questions to get them there.** We don't leave a student to figure it out alone and call that learning. If they're stuck, we ask a question that points at the next step, and we answer theirs. By the end of the session they should understand it. Being told to try harder doesn't count.

## The one thing Braden got wrong early

He thought explaining something the same way would work for every student. It doesn't. **The principles stay the same; the explanation changes.** If an explanation isn't landing, don't repeat it louder or slower. Try it another way: a picture, a smaller number, a different example, or start from what they already got right.

## The methods

These are Braden's own methods, and every sheet and session uses them. Here is each one at the depth you need today. The full method sheets are in the workspace, and Priya will show you how she uses them.

| Method | For | The steps |
|---|---|---|
| **calc-CARE** | every calculation | **C**lassify: what is it asking for, in what unit, rounded how? · **A**nalyze: set it up so the units cancel to the answer's unit · **R**eview: is the answer real, or is it 10 times off? · **E**xecute: commit, once |
| **STAT** | picking a statistics test | **S**pot the outcome's data type (a count, a rank or a measurement) · **T**ally the groups and whether they're the same people or different people · **A**nalyze the shape (normal or skewed, measurements only) · **T**est name: read it off the table |
| **CASE** | clinical case questions | **C**heck the question at the bottom first · **A**ssess what rules a drug out (allergies, kidneys, liver, pregnancy) · **S**elect first-line from what's left · **E**valuate for interactions and duplicates |
| **MAPP** | learning a drug class | the class pattern first and why it happens, then the main uses and pearls, then the detail later; understanding before coverage. This page gives MAPP's idea, not its letters; for those, see the full method sheet with Priya |

## How the sheets build

Every student gets the same start, then each topic climbs the same ladder.

1. **At enrollment:** a formula review and a diagnostic. The diagnostic tells us where they are before session one.
2. **Teaching sheet:** teaches before it asks. Worked examples, then questions.
3. **Practice sheet:** no hints. This shows what they can do alone.
4. **Re-drill:** only the kinds of problems they missed.
5. **Extra practice:** more reps, for a topic that's close but not solid.
6. **Quiz:** no formulas given. This shows what they've kept.

Every sheet has a **key**. The key is for tutors only and is never sent to a student. Homework is due 12 hours before the session, so there's time to grade it.

## How a session runs

Sessions are 60 minutes on Google Meet. Before each one, the tutor has a **run sheet**: the parts, the minutes for each, and what to cut first if you fall behind.

- **Open (about 3 minutes).** Tell them what they closed since last time; start with what they got right. Ask one check-in question, such as whether their exam is booked.
- **Their questions come first.** If they emailed about a problem, start there, ahead of the plan.
- **Each miss, the same way:**
  1. The problem.
  2. What happened: where each number in the question went.
  3. The fix, as one short rule.
  4. What they wrote next to what it should be.
  5. A fresh problem they work out loud while you ask questions.
- **No scores.** Show what they did next to what the question asks for; don't mark it wrong. Tell them the right answer only if they ask. The key itself is never sent.
- **Close (about 4 minutes).** Ask what's still fuzzy. Give one habit to take into the exam.
- **After.** Write the session record: each gap marked **got it**, **shaky** or **open**, and how the session compared with the run sheet. If you ran long or didn't reach a part, say so. That's normal, and the next session plans from it.

## What you don't do on your own

| Not on your own | Who to ask |
|---|---|
| Write a new sheet or change one in the library | Braden |
| Send anything to a student: email, homework, a recap | Braden; you leave a draft, he approves it |
| Change a key, even one you think is wrong | Braden; tell Priya too |
| Promise a pass, a score or a result, in any words | never; "more prepared" is as far as we go |
| Give advice about a real patient or the student's own medicines | never; we teach, we don't practice pharmacy |
