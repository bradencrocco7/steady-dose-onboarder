# A sample session

One job: the practice student's last session, on paper, so a new tutor can see how a session runs before they ever watch a live one. This is part 4 of your first day.

It has the run sheet Priya wrote before the session and the session record she wrote after. In the workspace, both are in `operations/students/practice-student/sessions/`. The practice student is made up. Everything else is how a session here really runs (`how-we-teach.md`). Shadowing a live session starts after day one.

## What to look for

1. She opens with what the student got right.
2. The student's own question comes before her plan.
3. Every miss gets the same five steps.
4. She never gives a score and never says "wrong".
5. She asks questions that point at the next step, and she answers theirs.
6. When she falls behind, she cuts the part the run sheet said to cut.

### Miss 1: ratio strength

1. The problem: "How many mg/mL is epinephrine 1:1,000?"
2. What happened: the 1 is 1 gram. They turned it into 100 mg, so 100 mg ÷ 1,000 mL.
3. The fix: "A ratio strength is grams in milliliters. 1:1,000 is 1 g in 1,000 mL, and 1 g is 1,000 mg."
4. What they wrote next to what it should be: 0.1 mg/mL, next to 1 mg/mL.
5. A fresh problem to work out loud: "How many mg/mL is 1:10,000?" (0.1 mg/mL)

### Miss 2: dose volume from a suspension

1. The problem: "The order is 300 mg. The suspension is 250 mg per 5 mL. How many mL?"
2. What happened: the ratio went in upside down, 250 ÷ 300 × 5.
3. The fix: "Write the unit on every number. If the units don't cancel down to mL, flip the ratio."
4. What they wrote next to what it should be: 4.2 mL, next to 6 mL.
5. A fresh problem to work out loud: "The order is 400 mg. The suspension is 200 mg per 5 mL." (10 mL)

### Miss 3: drip rate

1. The problem: "1,000 mL over 10 hours, with a 15 drops/mL set. How many drops per minute?"
2. What happened: the hours never became minutes, 1,000 ÷ 10 × 15.
3. The fix: "Change the time to the unit the answer asks for before you start."
4. What they wrote next to what it should be: 1,500 drops/min, next to 25 drops/min.
5. A fresh problem to work out loud: "500 mL over 4 hours, with a 20 drops/mL set." (42 drops/min)

## A minute of the session: miss 2

> **Priya:** Here's what you wrote: 250 ÷ 300 × 5. Read me the unit on each number.
>
> **Student:** 250 milligrams, 300 milligrams, and 5 mL.
>
> **Priya:** Good. What unit does the question want?
>
> **Student:** mL.
>
> **Priya:** So which unit has to cancel?
>
> **Student:** The milligrams. Oh. I've got them upside down. It should be 300 over 250.
>
> **Priya:** Try it that way.
>
> **Student:** 300 ÷ 250 × 5 is 6 mL.
>
> **Priya:** Does 6 mL make sense? The order is a bit more than what's in one 5 mL spoonful.
>
> **Student:** Yes. A bit more than 5.
>
> **Priya:** That's it. New one, out loud this time: the order is 400 mg, and the suspension is 200 mg per 5 mL.

## Where each one shows up

Try to find all six yourself first. Then check:

1. The open leads with percent strength, which they closed since session 1.
2. Their rounding question took the first 15 minutes, ahead of miss 1.
3. Misses 1, 2 and 3 are each planned as the same five steps.
4. In the minute of the session, Priya never says "wrong". She reads back what the student wrote.
5. In the same minute: "What unit does the question want?" and "So which unit has to cancel?"
6. Miss 3 was cut, as the run sheet said, and it's marked open for next time.
